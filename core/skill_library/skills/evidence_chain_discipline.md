# Skill: Evidence Chain Discipline

## Purpose

Prevent a polished concept, essay or prototype from being supported by an invisible or overstated reasoning chain.

## Use when

- a project moves from research into a claim or design decision;
- an insight needs to be connected to criteria, a concept or a prototype;
- a test result is being used to justify iteration or impact;
- a document or presentation needs an evidence-first audit.

## Core chain

```text
source
  → note
  → interpretation
  → claim
  → evidence / counterpoint / limitation
  → section or design decision
  → prototype or test
  → iteration
  → claimed impact
```

Every arrow is a question to answer, not a decorative diagram.

## Operating rules

1. Name the source type and provenance.
2. Keep descriptive observation separate from interpretation.
3. Keep participant or stakeholder accounts separate from researcher synthesis.
4. State whether a claim is supported, provisional, disputed or missing.
5. Link each major design decision to admitted evidence and rejected alternatives.
6. Record what a prototype was intended to learn before describing what it shows.
7. State test context, sample or interaction count, method, observation and limitation.
8. Do not turn positive reaction, a mock artefact or a single account into proof of effectiveness.
9. Label hypotheses, desired futures and design propositions explicitly.
10. Preserve uncertainty rather than filling it with plausible language.

## Minimum claim record

```markdown
### Claim
- Statement:
- Claim status: supported / provisional / disputed / missing
- Source or evidence:
- Interpretation:
- Counterpoint or limitation:
- Consequence for the project:
- Next verification:
```

## Testing check

Before calling something evidence, record:

- what the encounter or test was meant to reveal;
- who or what produced the material and under what conditions;
- what was observed or said;
- what remains interpretation;
- what changed as a result;
- what the test cannot establish.

## Public and ethical boundary

Do not expose identities, contact details, consent records or identifiable raw material in a shared repository. Synthetic or mock data must be labelled as such. A method improves reasoning; it does not grant permission to publish another person's material.
