# Reflection Document Guidance Video Analysis

Status: reusable analysis protocol; not a copy of a course video or displayed student work
Scope: analysing a programme-provided video that explains how to prepare a Stage 3 reflection document

This tool turns spoken guidance, displayed examples and visual/layout
demonstrations into a traceable, assessment-aware working plan. It is designed
for a user's own project and must not import that project into the reusable
Threading core.

## What the analysis must establish

The analysis should distinguish:

1. what the programme explicitly requires;
2. what the presenter recommends rather than requires;
3. why a displayed example is being shown;
4. how reflection is communicated through writing, images, sequencing and layout;
5. which principles are transferable to the user's document;
6. where video guidance agrees with, extends or conflicts with current official
   assessment sources;
7. which concrete writing, evidence and page-design actions follow.

Use this bridge:

```text
video/audio + subtitle + displayed example
→ source-aligned observation
→ classified guidance
→ interpretation with confidence
→ official assessment cross-check
→ project mapping
→ document action / gap / layout decision
```

## Assessment authority and evidence classes

Read the pack's [`stage3-assessment-guide.md`](../assessment/stage3-assessment-guide.md)
before interpreting the video. The current official assessment form and the five
ILOs outrank historical video guidance.

Classify every extracted point:

| Class | Meaning | Permitted use |
|---|---|---|
| `FORMAL REQUIREMENT` | Explicit requirement supported by current written authority | Treat as mandatory and cite the written source plus timestamp |
| `VIDEO INSTRUCTION` | Direct instruction or clear expectation from the presenter | Treat as important dated guidance, not automatically a formal rule |
| `VIDEO RECOMMENDATION` | Advice, preference or suggested practice | Use as guidance and explain its likely benefit |
| `EXAMPLE OBSERVATION` | Directly visible or spoken feature of an example | Describe only what can actually be seen or heard |
| `PRESENTER EVALUATION` | Presenter praises, criticises or explains a specific feature | Attach the evaluation only to that feature |
| `TRANSFERABLE PATTERN` | Reusable principle inferred from one or more examples | Label it as interpretation and state its support |
| `PROJECT APPLICATION` | Proposed action for the user's own document | Tie it to project evidence, an ILO or a documented gap |
| `UNCERTAIN` | Wording, image, attribution or implication cannot be confirmed | Do not promote it into a requirement or claim |

Do not infer that the presenter approves an entire example merely because it
appears in the video.

## Inputs and privacy

Required:

- original video or an authorised local pointer;
- local subtitle file, preferably `.srt` or `.vtt` with timestamps.

Helpful but optional:

- original slides, handout or PDF used in the session;
- written description distributed with the video;
- the user's reflection-document draft or Figma `Document` page;
- preferred final-document format and working language.

Keep the video, subtitles, extracted frames and displayed student examples in a
user-controlled local source area. Do not commit course recordings, student
identities, screenshots, raw transcripts or restricted examples to the reusable
pack unless reuse permission is explicit.

## Analysis workflow

### 1. Intake and file integrity

Record source filename or pointer, format, size, duration, modification date and
known provenance. Preserve the original files unchanged. Keep derived audio,
frames, crops and notes in separate folders.

### 2. Subtitle-first calibration

Compare subtitle, audio and picture at a small sample from the opening, a middle
section, an example-dense section and the closing. Record missing speech, timing
drift, academic terms, names, speaker changes and whether subtitles match the
displayed example. Classify the subtitle as `high`, `usable with targeted
verification` or `unreliable`.

Use Whisper or another transcription fallback only when subtitles omit speech,
timestamps drift, a term is unclear or exact wording materially affects an
assessment interpretation. Do not transcribe the full recording by default.

### 3. Structural segmentation

Build a timestamped timeline that distinguishes, as applicable:

- introduction and purpose;
- formal requirements;
- proposed document structure;
- writing and reflection guidance;
- visual communication and layout guidance;
- displayed student examples;
- warnings and common mistakes;
- practical submission advice;
- grading or summary material.

The recurring teaching-facing categories are:

- Research & Criticality;
- Development & Methods;
- Ideation & Creative Iteration;
- Reflection & Collaboration;
- Communication.

Treat these as a navigation checklist, then cross-check them against the current
official rubric and ILOs.

Use this neutral orientation table when segmenting the video; do not treat it as
a replacement for the current assessment form:

| Teaching-facing category | Questions to locate in the video/document |
|---|---|
| Research & Criticality | What context, theories, debates, stakeholders and research findings are made visible? |
| Development & Methods | Why were methods chosen, how were they delivered, and how were findings interpreted? |
| Ideation & Creative Iteration | How did opportunities become concepts, alternatives, prototypes, feedback and changes? |
| Reflection & Collaboration | What was learned, challenged or changed through practice, ethics, positionality and collaboration? |
| Communication | Is the argument navigable, visually hierarchical, professionally resolved and accessible to non-specialists? |

### 4. Frame and page-state reconstruction

For each meaningful segment, capture a representative frame and identify whether
it shows a lecturer slide, student document page, page sequence or another
artefact. Capture additional states when text, annotations, animation or
scrolling changes. Link every frame to a timestamp and subtitle segment.

Do not claim exact fonts, measurements, colours or grid rules unless the image or
source file supports that level of precision.

### 5. Spoken-guidance record

Use one row per meaningful point:

| Time | Source wording | Speaker status | Evidence class | Topic | Visual context | Interpretation | Confidence | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:00 | short quote or faithful paraphrase | known / unknown / uncertain | one class above | requirement / reflection / evidence / layout / etc. | frame or page | bounded meaning | high / medium / low | subtitle / audio / slide / written source |

Use short quotations only when exact wording matters. Prefer source-linked
paraphrase for the analytical record.

### 6. Example analysis

For each displayed example, record:

- timestamp range and anonymous example label;
- visible page count and sequence;
- directly observable headings, images, diagrams, evidence, annotations,
  citations and source labels;
- relationship between text and image, hierarchy, density and whitespace;
- what happened, why a method or decision was chosen, what was learned, what
  changed, what failed and what remained unresolved;
- ethics, positionality, collaboration and limitation signals;
- exactly what the presenter praised, criticised or explained;
- transferable principle, supporting observation and risk of copying surface
  style without its evidential function.

Separate descriptive process documentation from critical reflection.

### 7. Official cross-check

Compare each important video point with:

- ILO-01 through ILO-05;
- the current document requirements and rubric dimensions;
- the current presentation requirements when relevant;
- current written programme guidance.

Use this matrix:

| Video point | Video class | Formal source support | ILO/rubric mapping | Agreement or conflict | Operational implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |  |

A topic mention does not satisfy an ILO. The document must show visible evidence,
interpretation and consequence.

### 8. Map to the user's project

Only after permission, make a bounded pass over the user's own project records.
For each proposed document section or page, record:

- reflection or claim purpose;
- available evidence and its type;
- evidence limitation;
- decision or design change supported;
- relevant ILO and rubric dimension;
- suggested visual material;
- ready / develop / verify / gather / do-not-claim status;
- one next action.

Do not convert limited testing, mock data, protocols, invitations or unbuilt
mechanisms into demonstrated impact.

## Required outputs

1. **Source and timeline index** — full duration, sections, examples and frame references.
2. **Guidance and authority matrix** — requirements, instructions, recommendations,
   warnings and inferred principles with written-source cross-checks.
3. **Example and visual-pattern atlas** — observable content, page sequence,
   reflection function, commentary and transferable principles.
4. **Reflection-document application map** — guidance and official requirements
   mapped to the user's evidence, decisions, prototypes, testing and gaps.
5. **Writing and page-design plan** — section purpose, ILO coverage, source
   material, visual role and priority; this is a plan, not invented prose.
6. **Verification and uncertainty log** — subtitle errors, unreadable frames,
   uncertain interpretations, source conflicts, missing permissions and evidence
   still needed.

## Quality gate

Before treating the analysis as complete, verify that:

- the entire video duration is represented;
- every major example has a timestamp and representative frame;
- animated or scrolling examples have enough captured states;
- subtitle quality was sampled rather than assumed;
- important wording was audio-checked where needed;
- observation is separated from interpretation;
- formal requirements are separated from recommendations and examples;
- presenter praise is attached only to the discussed feature;
- current official PDFs and all five ILOs were used as the assessment gate;
- every project application names supporting evidence and limitations;
- no limited test or mock artefact is presented as validation;
- student identities and restricted course material remain protected;
- uncertainty and conflicts are explicit;
- outputs end in concrete document actions.
