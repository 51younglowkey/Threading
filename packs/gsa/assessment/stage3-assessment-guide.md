# Stage 3 Assessment Guide

Status: operational guide derived from user-authorised Stage 3 assessment forms
Scope: Design Innovation-related Stage 3 document and presentation review
Authority: the current official assessment form for the relevant cohort always outranks this guide

This file makes the assessment logic usable inside Threading. It is not an
official GSA assessment form, does not assign a new numerical weighting and does
not replace the current PDF supplied by the programme.

The ILOs and rubric-derived terms are summarised from the original assessment
source. This file's organisation and operational prompts do not create a new
institutional policy or licence. It is intentionally not a transcription of the
official wording.

This is the programme-level assessment scaffold. It does not include a user's
project-specific design-criterion IDs; those belong in the user-owned project
profile and must be derived from that project's evidence.

## Authority order

Use this order when sources disagree:

1. the five full Stage 3 Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs);
2. the current official Stage 3 assessment PDF for the deliverable;
3. the official rubric dimensions and grade descriptors in that PDF;
4. current written programme or tutor guidance;
5. historical teaching videos and displayed examples;
6. the Agent's interpretation.

Record conflicts instead of resolving them by assumption. A historical video can
explain an expectation, but it cannot silently update the current assessment
standard.

Expected local source names (not bundled):

- `Stage 3 PD Assessment Form.pdf` — final reflection document;
- `Stage 3 Presentation Assessment Form.pdf` — presentation.

## Five Stage 3 ILOs

The following are operational paraphrases of the five ILOs. Keep the current
official form as the source of truth for exact wording.

### ILO-01 — Critical understanding of context and impact

Examine a project context critically through relevant theories and global/local
debates, making the possible direction and socio-economic, environmental and/or
technological impact visible.

### ILO-02 — Critical application of research methods

Plan and critically use appropriate primary and secondary research methods from
Design Innovation or relevant disciplines to explore and interpret a context and
support project development.

### ILO-03 — Development, iteration and testing

Develop, iterate and test appropriate innovative proposals at different levels of
resolution and through different media, responding to an identified demand or
opportunity in context.

### ILO-04 — Professional communication

Communicate research insights, proposals, outcomes and their potential impact to
specialist and non-specialist audiences using appropriate media and a professional
standard of clarity and resolution.

### ILO-05 — Critical reflection, ethics and collaboration

Reflect critically on project interactions, ethics, personal practice and
collaboration, and explain what these reveal about the aims, activities and
potential impact of a design-led innovation outcome or approach.

## Final reflection document

The official document guidance currently represented by this pack describes the
reflection document as:

- 75% of the credits;
- 6,000–10,000 words;
- a detailed, self-reflective visual document;
- an account of the process and appropriate use of methods;
- an outcome showing value and potential impact in response to opportunities or
  unmet needs;
- an explanation of the context and how research informed the process rather
  than assumption.

The document rubric dimensions are:

1. **Research Methods** — appropriate methods, rationale, grounding and use;
2. **Understanding of Context** — global/local context, evidence, opportunities
   or unmet needs, and impact on relevant stakeholders;
3. **Ideation & Iteration** — creative exploration, alternatives, development,
   iteration and final proposal;
4. **Reflection & Collaboration** — collaboration, positionality, ethics,
   process, learning and future impact;
5. **Communication** — resolution, professional standard, relevance, hierarchy
   and accessibility to non-specialists;
6. **Overall Grade** — holistic judgement across the ILOs and evidence.

Use these operational cues when checking whether each dimension is visible:

| Dimension | Look for visible evidence of |
|---|---|
| Research Methods | Appropriate research tools, clear grounding, method rationale, interpretation and limits |
| Understanding of Context | Global and local context, relevant evidence, identified opportunities or unmet needs, and impact on stakeholders |
| Ideation & Iteration | Creative exploration, alternatives, iterative development and a final proposal that answers the identified opportunity or need |
| Reflection & Collaboration | External collaboration where relevant, positionality, ethics, process, learning and potential future impact |
| Communication | Professional resolution, clear hierarchy, contextual relevance, potential impact and accessibility to non-specialists |

## Stage 3 presentation

The official presentation form currently represented by this pack describes a
25% credit, 10-minute presentation. It should be a concise and visual account of
the process and outcome, including the key research findings, insights,
opportunity and pursued proposal.

The presentation-specific requirements are to:

- explain unmet needs identified through design research and their impact on key
  stakeholder groups or individuals;
- apply appropriate primary and secondary research methods to understand the
  context or issue;
- develop and communicate an innovative proposal at an appropriate degree of
  resolution for a non-specialist audience.

The presentation rubric dimensions are:

1. **Understanding**;
2. **Justification**;
3. **Impact**;
4. **Communication**;
5. **Overall Grade**.

Use these presentation cues:

| Dimension | Look for visible evidence of |
|---|---|
| Understanding | Context at global and local levels, evidence, opportunities or unmet needs, and stakeholder impact |
| Justification | Relevant primary and secondary research insights informing the final outcome |
| Impact | Explanation of the proposal's impact or potential impact and benefit |
| Communication | Appropriate resolution, professional standard, relevance, contextual support and non-specialist accessibility |

These presentation requirements do not replace the five full ILOs. Audit both.

## Grade bands and score codes

The official forms use these grade codes:

| Band | Codes | Operational meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Excellent | A1–A5 (22–18) | Exemplary range and depth, comprehensive relevant material and analyses, and discriminating judgement |
| Very Good | B1–B3 (17–15) | Virtually all ILOs attained, with close familiarity with a wide range of evidence and appreciable depth |
| Good | C1–C3 (14–12) | Most ILOs attained, with a circumscribed evidence base and variable depth |
| Satisfactory | D1–D3 (11–9) | Acceptable attainment with a minimally sufficient evidence base and generally reasonable but insecure analysis |
| Weak | E1–E3 (8–6) | Specific ILO deficiencies, mixed evidence and weak argument or handling |
| Poor | F1–F3 (5–3) | ILO attainment appreciably deficient in critical respects, without a secure factual or analytical basis |
| Very Poor | G1–G2 (2–1) | ILO attainment markedly deficient, with irrelevant, incomplete or flawed treatment |
| No convincing evidence | H (0) | No convincing evidence of attainment; treatment is directionless and fragmentary |
| Compliance failure | CR | Failure to comply with published requirements without good cause |

These descriptors are qualitative aids, not a formula for predicting a grade.
Do not invent a numerical weighting for individual ILOs unless the current
official form explicitly supplies one.

## Mandatory audit

Use this matrix for every document, presentation, chapter, script or final
export:

| ILO | Status | Visible evidence location | What it demonstrates | Gap / risk | Required revision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ILO-01 | Met / Partial / Missing |  | Context, debate, direction and impact |  |  |
| ILO-02 | Met / Partial / Missing |  | Planned and critically applied methods |  |  |
| ILO-03 | Met / Partial / Missing |  | Development, iteration, testing and resolution |  |  |
| ILO-04 | Met / Partial / Missing |  | Professional communication for intended audiences |  |  |
| ILO-05 | Met / Partial / Missing |  | Reflection, ethics, practice, collaboration and impact |  |  |

Rules:

- A topic mention is not enough; the artefact must show evidence,
  interpretation and consequence.
- `Met` requires visible, traceable support in the artefact being reviewed.
- `Partial` means relevant material exists but is incomplete, implicit, weakly
  connected or unclear.
- `Missing` means an assessor cannot locate defensible evidence in the artefact.
- Do not treat a planned test, mock data, protocol or unbuilt mechanism as
  demonstrated impact.

## Source boundary

The official assessment PDFs are not bundled in this pack. Keep them in an
authorised local source location and verify exact wording, layout and current
grade-band detail before a consequential assessment decision. The pack provides
an operational paraphrase and audit scaffold, not institutional authority.
