# Threading Workflow Rules

These rules govern the reusable core. They apply to academic, design-research and practice-based projects added later by individual users.

## Evidence discipline

- Keep source evidence, raw notes, cleaned notes, interpretation, synthesis and final claims in visibly different layers.
- Preserve the chain `source -> note -> interpretation -> claim -> evidence -> limitation -> output`.
- Preserve the design chain `evidence -> insight -> opportunity -> criteria -> concept -> prototype -> testing -> iteration -> claimed impact`.
- Keep quotations exact, anonymised where required and clearly labelled as quotations.
- Never present a paraphrase, machine transcript, synthetic example or mock response as a verbatim source.
- State sample size, evidence type, uncertainty and limits when they affect a claim.
- Mark unsupported material with `[EVIDENCE NEEDED]` or `[SOURCE TO VERIFY]`; do not fill gaps with plausible prose.

## Method discipline

- A taught method is evidence of methodological capability and a tool for analysis; it is not empirical proof about the project topic.
- Choose a method because it resolves a named analytical need, not because it sounds sophisticated.
- State inputs, method steps, analytical outputs, assumptions, risks and the next verification step.
- Label inferred causal links as `analytical hypothesis` and desired futures as `design proposition`.
- Use the smallest combination of methods that makes the reasoning visible.

## Research and testing

- Define what a method or prototype can and cannot reveal before collecting material.
- Keep participant response, researcher interpretation and later design decision separate.
- A prototype without a testing record is a representation, not evidence of effectiveness.
- Do not claim adoption, learning impact, behaviour change or systemic change without matching evidence.
- Obtain appropriate consent and ethical approval before collecting identifiable research material.

## Personal and public boundaries

- Personal names, contact details, schedules, correspondence, consent records, raw recordings, identifiable images and institution-restricted documents remain outside the reusable core.
- User-owned project profiles should use their own local storage and should not be copied into the public edition by default.
- Neutral examples must be labelled as examples; synthetic or mock data must be labelled as synthetic or mock.
- Review both the working tree and Git history before external publication.

## Iteration and records

- Record why a change was made, what informed it, what was tested and what remains uncertain.
- Keep dated historical records when they explain a decision; do not rewrite history to make a project look more certain than it was.
- Templates are scaffolds, not evidence. Scripts are tools, not authority.
